Waiting, and the desktop's devices

Mockup for the review of 3 November. Part A redesigns Waiting on both apps. Part B makes the desktop's device circle work as the phone's does. Grey words in the captions explain; they aren't on screen.

A. Waiting, with previews

How others do it. Google Photos puts a progress ring around the profile picture while it backs up, and its backup page shows the photo going up right now next to how many are left; queued photos get a small badge on their own thumbnail. WhatsApp and Telegram show the file itself with a ring over it and × to stop. Quick Share rings the recipient's picture.

The change: no icons, and no kinds of job. Waiting has one row per device that files are going to or coming from, with an arrow for which way. Backups, new files and sends to the same device are one stack of previews, the one moving now on top with a ring over it. A number, a size, and a chip only when it's waiting on something (Off, Open the app).

B. The desktop's devices, as on the phone

Today the circle opens a dropdown where clicking a name chooses it and the › beside it opens its page, two actions in one row. The phone does it more simply, so the desktop copies the phone exactly: click the circle → that device's page. On the page, Switch device (blue) opens the list to choose another, just as holding the circle does on the phone. On the desktop, right-clicking the circle opens the list directly, the desktop's version of a long press. The page itself is the phone's: picture, name, On/Off and ⓘ, then one card of what it keeps here, then Remove and Switch device.
