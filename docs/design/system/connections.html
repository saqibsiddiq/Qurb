The states a device can be inSeen from one side: what the computer knows about a phone, or the phone about the computer.

Not pairedNever met. Nothing shared.
PairingA code is showing; one side has scanned it; both sides compare six digits.
Paired, onTrusted, and reachable now: directly, or through Tor.
Paired, offTrusted, not reachable now: asleep, app closed, no network. Waits; nothing is lost.
RemovedTrust ended on both sides. Its files kept here, locked, and remembered as its, unless deleted.
Not paired → Pairing a code is shown and scanned · Pairing → Paired both approve · Pairing → Not paired declined, expired, five wrong tries
On ⇄ Off by itself, as devices sleep, wake and move between networks · Paired → Removed either side removes the other
Removed → Paired the same device pairs again, and gets its files back · Removed → Not paired its files deleted too, or forgotten for good

To buildIn this order. Each a decision record, then tests, then tried on the owner's devices.

1
Remove ends trust, and remembers who. The device's record stays, marked removed, with its fingerprint and device id; it is refused, listed nowhere but its locked files.
2
The same device, recognised. A pairing presenting a removed device's identity is told it is that device (as Request::Become answers today for one not removed); the approval says "is back" and its files are its again.
3
Told it was removed. The remover sends a signed "you were removed" when it can, and refuses with a distinct answer when it can't, so the removed side learns either way and says so instead of showing "off" for ever.
4
A removed device's files in storage. Listed under its name in This computer's storage, locked, with Delete; never deleted by themselves.
5
A computer set up again, recognised. A permanent identity for computers too, derived from the key and the machine, as phones have; until then, "the same as another device".
